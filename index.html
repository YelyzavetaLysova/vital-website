The challenge

Health information travels fast.
Context often does not.

01

Claims are everywhere

Food, fitness, body image, treatments and public-health topics appear throughout news and social media.

02

Context gets lost

Uncertainty, study design, populations and important limitations may disappear as research becomes a headline.

03

Advertorial content

Health claims can be part of paid or commercially influenced content and require careful interpretation.

04

Real-world harm

Health misinformation can affect vaccination, diets, treatments, trust and collective public-health decisions.

Why this matters now

More sources.
Higher stakes.

Health information increasingly reaches people through social media, aggregators and AI alongside traditional journalism. When context, evidence and editorial safeguards are missing, misinformation can affect individual decisions, public health and trust in institutions.

01 A changing information landscape

AI assistants, social platforms, video networks and aggregators are becoming important routes through which people encounter and interpret health information.

02 Better context, better decisions

Clear evidence, limitations and alternative sources can help people recognise unsupported claims and make more informed decisions about health.

03 Trust matters

Transparent evidence communication can support trust in journalism, health authorities and public institutions while improving resilience to misinformation.
